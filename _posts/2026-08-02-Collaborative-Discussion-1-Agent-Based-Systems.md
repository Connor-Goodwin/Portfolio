Agent Based Systems are not a new concept in the world of computing but recent advances in Artificial Intelligence (AI) technology have led to the pairing of Large Language Models (LLMs) with the traditional agent systems. This has taken them from being rule based tools capable of completing basic tasks to having the ability to autonomously handle complex tasks. (Chopra, 2025)
These developments have led to the creation of a new lucrative industry built around these AI agent systems which was worth $4.35bn in 2025 and forecasted to grow to $103bn by 2034. (Chopra, 2025) This growth has been driven by the high rate of adoption by businesses with a McKinsey & Company survey in 2025 finding that 62% of companies were already experimenting with AI agents in some capacity. (McKinsey & Company, 2025)
This growth has also been driven partly by the breadth of applications these agents can be utilised for across nearly all industries. Common examples include autonomous trading agents in finance, clinical assistants in healthcare and intelligent tutoring systems in education. (Zuo, 2025) This versatility is a key benefit to organisations for using these agent systems as they can be adapted to automate a variety of industry-specific tasks to provide them with faster decision making and the ability to deploy automation with limited human oversight.
